## Getting started

**What does the PhD look like, and how long does it take?**
Coursework typically takes two years, followed by the [comprehensive exam](policies/comprehensive-exam/), the [proposal defense](policies/proposal-defense/), advancement to candidacy, and the dissertation. Most students finish in five to six years. The program is full-time and in residence; there is no part-time or hybrid track, and funded students must enroll in at least 9 hours each fall and spring.

**How many credit hours do I need?**
The degree is built from required core courses (Planning Theory, Research Design, and the doctoral Colloquium), two advanced methods courses (one qualitative, one quantitative), courses in your planning specialization, courses outside planning, and dissertation hours. See your Program of Work sheet and the PhD Handbook for the current total and the breakdown; the sheet is the authoritative record of where you stand.

**What is the Program of Work sheet, and the Milestone Agreement?**
Each student has a personal Google Sheet, maintained with the PhD Program Coordinator, that records your courses, specialization, methods approvals, equivalency plan, and milestone dates. You'll set it up with your advisor and the Coordinator in your first semester and are expected to keep it current as you register. It replaces the paper Program of Work and Milestone Agreement forms in the older handbook. Ask the Coordinator for the link to yours.

**What is "master's equivalency," and does it apply to me?**
The PhD assumes a U.S. master's degree in planning. If you enter without one, the PhD Program Coordinator and your advisor will look at your transcripts during orientation and may ask you to take a small number of master's-level planning courses (typically up to three or four) so you have the disciplinary grounding the rest of the program builds on. Planning Law is a common example for students whose degree is from outside the U.S.; Planning History and Theory, GIS, or land use planning are others. The plan is tailored to you; almost no one is asked to take the full MSCRP core. Two rules to know: equivalency courses **do not count** toward your PhD hours or satisfy PhD requirements (a master's-level methods course does not meet the advanced methods requirement), and the Coordinator may waive an equivalency course on review of a syllabus. A written policy on master's equivalency is being drafted for GSC review in fall 2026.

**Can courses from my master's degree transfer or waive PhD requirements?**
Almost never. PhD coursework is where you build relationships with the faculty who will serve on your committees, and standards vary across institutions. Courses that satisfied your master's cannot also count toward the PhD. Bring your transcripts to your orientation meeting anyway; they inform the equivalency conversation above.

**Who do I meet with when I arrive?**
Book an orientation meeting with the PhD Program Coordinator and your advisor in the first weeks of the semester. You'll set up your Program of Work sheet, settle any equivalency plan, and map out your first two years. Read the PhD Handbook beforehand.

## Coursework and methods

**What counts as an advanced qualitative methods course?**
The requirement is meant to give you hands-on training in collecting and analyzing data that isn't primarily numerical: ethnography, case study, archival, interview, and focus-group methods, usually with some qualitative coding. A course with "qualitative methods" in the title will almost certainly qualify; a survey design course probably won't. The [recommended list](policies/methods-requirements/) is a starting point but is out of date; several listed courses are no longer offered. To get a course approved, send the PhD Program Coordinator the **syllabus** (not just the catalog description) before you enroll, and enroll in the graduate section.

**What counts as an advanced quantitative methods course?**
Ideally a course focused on a specific advanced technique: machine learning, structural equation modeling, discrete choice modeling, advanced regression or econometrics, longitudinal or spatial analysis. A general introductory statistics course largely duplicates the MSCRP quantitative methods course and is a weaker choice, though it can be approved. Same process: syllabus to the Coordinator before enrolling. See the [recommended list](policies/methods-requirements/), with the same caveat about currency.

**How do I find methods courses in other departments, and how do I get in?**
Ask other PhD students what they've taken; they're the best current source. Then email the instructor directly rather than sitting on a waitlist. Most instructors admit outside doctoral students when asked.

**Can independent study count toward my specialization?**
One independent study with a CRP core faculty member may count toward the 12-hour planning specialization. The Graduate School caps independent study at 6 credits toward degree requirements, and program guidance limits it to about 20 percent of your coursework. Students often use an independent study in the semester before comps to read for the exam with a committee member.

**Is the doctoral Colloquium offered every year?**
It is offered when the incoming cohort is large enough to sustain it. It will be offered at least every other year. In years with a very small cohort the Coordinator will arrange an alternative; you will not be penalized for a year it isn't offered.

## Comprehensive exam

The governing document is the [Comprehensive Exam Policy](policies/comprehensive-exam/). Its "Who Does What" section and the checklist at the end answer most procedural questions. Highlights:

**What is the format?**
Four written essays over 10 days: Planning Theory, Research Design, Planning Specialization, and Dissertation Topic. Each area offers two or three questions and you answer one. Questions are tied to your approved reading list, and theory and research design questions are written to connect to your specialization. You receive the exam by email from phdcrp@utexas.edu and return the essays electronically, in both Word and PDF, to the PhD Program Coordinator by the deadline.

**When can I take it?**
When your coursework is complete or nearly so (check with the Coordinator if you're finishing a course), your committee is formed, and your reading list is final. There are no fixed exam periods; most students schedule near the beginning or end of a semester when faculty are available. Tell the Coordinator at least a semester ahead. The reading list should be in draft about three months before and final about two months before.

**Who writes and grades the questions?**
Your advisor leads. Committee members who are CRP-GSC faculty propose questions in all four areas; the Planning Theory and Research Design instructors always contribute to Areas 1 and 2; external members contribute in Areas 3 and/or 4. GSC readers grade all four essays; external members' grades are advisory. Readers have four weeks; the Coordinator synthesizes the grades into a results letter within five.

**Do I need paperwork to put someone on my comps committee?**
No. There is no Graduate School form at this stage; the committee is formalized later, at candidacy. Your advisor sends the Coordinator the roster before the exam is scheduled.

**How do I submit revisions?**
One round of revisions is allowed. Send the Coordinator a tracked-changes version and a clean version of each revised essay, in Word. Combined or separate files are both fine. Readers have two weeks to grade revisions.

## Committee, proposal, and candidacy

The governing document is the [Proposal Defense Policy](policies/proposal-defense/). Highlights:

**How soon after comps must I defend my proposal?**
Within two full semesters (summers don't count). Your committee can't make its final form until after the defense, so use the gap to firm up membership.

**Can a UT faculty member outside CRP serve on my dissertation committee?**
Yes, and you need at least one such member. If they are UT faculty but not a CRP-GSC member, first confirm they're willing, then you or they email the CRP-GSC chair (cc your advisor and the Coordinator) asking to be added to the CRP GSC for the purpose of serving; the GSC votes at its next meeting. External members from outside UT need a terminal degree; you'll submit their CV and a "no-expense" letter to the Graduate School when you form the official committee.

**Who signs my candidacy application?**
The Graduate Advisor signs in the Graduate School's online system, after checking with the PhD Program Coordinator that your requirements are met. You'll be asked to enter a short (under 60 lines) description of your dissertation; have your advisor approve it first, and draft it offline because the form times out.

**How do I learn the outcome of my proposal defense revisions?**
The Coordinator emails you (cc your advisor) once the committee has approved your revisions. You may then apply for candidacy.

**My candidacy is about to expire. What happens?**
The Graduate School expects the dissertation within two years of candidacy. Extensions require a CRP-GSC vote: your chair submits a justification, the GSC chair collects votes, and the Graduate Advisor approves the extension in the routing system. Start this well before the deadline.

## Dissertation and defense

The governing document is the [Dissertation Defense Policy](policies/dissertation-defense/); for the three-article format, see the [Three-Article Dissertation Policy](policies/three-article-dissertation/). Highlights:

**Where are the defense requirements and forms?**
In the [Dissertation Defense Policy](policies/dissertation-defense/), Section 6, which lists every step: registration in a 99W course, room booking, the four-week submission to the committee and Coordinator, the two required chair emails, and the Request for Final Oral Examination. Don't confuse it with the proposal defense policy.

**Do I use the online system or the PDF for the Request for Final Oral Examination, and why does it list the Graduate Advisor?**
The Graduate School's form is signed by the Graduate Advisor, not the PhD Program Coordinator; that is how the School of Architecture is set up. Coordinate with the UTSOA Graduate Program Coordinator ([utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu](mailto:utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu)) to get the form into DocuSign well ahead of time; committee signatures take a while.

**Does the PhD Program Coordinator have to attend my defense?**
The policy asks that a defense be scheduled when the chair, committee, and PhD Program Coordinator can be present, and that the Coordinator receive the dissertation four weeks ahead so the defense can be announced. Send the Coordinator your proposed date at early as possible. The coordinator will attend when possible but their attendance at the defense is not strictly necessary.

**Can my defense, or a committee member, be remote?**
The Graduate School allows virtual defenses, and a committee member may join remotely in extenuating circumstances, but the program strongly prefers an in-person defense and expects the student and chair to work toward one.

**How long should my presentation be?**
The policy says roughly 35–40 minutes; committees often ask for shorter, around 25–30, to leave time for questions. Check with your chair. Make sure your references are complete and your citation style consistent; committees have also begun asking for a brief statement on any use of generative AI.

## Funding, travel, and employment

**How do I get conference travel funding, and how much is available?**
Three sources, all coordinated by the PhD Program Coordinator, who advertises them together each fall and spring:

1. **UTSOA Dean's conference support.** Every CRP PhD student is intended to receive one travel award of $1,300 during their time in the program, preferably to present an accepted paper. If your faculty PI's grant covers part of a trip, this fund can top it up to $1,300.
2. **Graduate School Professional Development Awards.** A small annual pool (about $1,100 for the program) for students presenting original UT research. Requires proof of acceptance, documentation of travel, and a short post-conference report; a registration hold can follow if the report isn't submitted.
3. **CRP program funds**, as available.

To apply, send the Coordinator the Student Professional Development form, your abstract, and proof of acceptance. Priority goes to students presenting original, peer-reviewed work, to those who haven't yet had a funded trip, and to those on the job market. Applying once covers all three sources; the Coordinator routes it. Apply for the ACSP Doctoral Workshop separately (deadline usually August).

**Can I hold a summer GRA and also keep a fellowship or scholarship?**
You can shift to another source of support as long as your total support doesn't fall below what your offer letter promised; the program avoids double-paying from school resources. Fellowship enrollment requirements can sometimes be waived by the Coordinator. A summer GRA usually requires enrolling in an independent study, which need not carry deliverables.

**Who gets TA positions after the funding guarantee ends?**
Funding-letter guarantees are filled first. After that, students without support are matched to open TA and GRA lines as they arise. Tell the Coordinator early if you'll need support beyond your guaranteed years.

**My pay changed when I moved between a TA and a GRA appointment. Why?**
TA and GRA rates differ. If your pay doesn't match your appointment letter, tell the Coordinator as soon as possible.

## Registration and logistics

**Do I have to enroll in the summer?**
Generally no, unless your funding requires it (a summer GRA or fellowship). International students should confirm their situation with Texas Global.

**What is the annual progress review?**
Each spring the PhD Program Coordinator meets with every student and their advisor. Beforehand, update your Program of Work sheet and complete the online Report of Progress form. If your advisor has several students, each meeting is separate.

**How do I get a desk in the PhD room, and a key?**
Desks in the PhD room are informally managed by the students who use it; check the room map in the PhD room folder and ask the Coordinator at the start of the semester if you want a carrel. Keys to the room are requested at the GOL front desk.

**How do I get printing credit?**
New PhD students send their EID to the UTSOA Graduate Program Coordinator ([utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu](mailto:utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu)) to have UT Print credit added to their library print account.

**How do I book a room for a defense?**
Through the UTSOA front desk (512-471-1922). The Dean's Conference Room is the usual choice; update your calendar invite with the location once confirmed.

**I missed a registration deadline and have a late fee.**
Registration and fees are handled by the UTSOA Graduate Program Coordinator ([utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu](mailto:utsoa-gradcoordinator@utexas.edu)), not the PhD Program Coordinator. Contact them first; in rare cases the program has helped, but you should not count on it.

**I didn't get an announcement or reimbursement email.**
Notices go to your official UT address (`@my.utexas.edu`). Check it or set up forwarding.

## International students

The program's academic requirements are the same for everyone. Things that come up: master's-level Planning Law is often part of an equivalency plan for students whose degree is from outside the U.S. (see master's equivalency above); summer enrollment is usually not required but confirm with Texas Global; the internship course (CRP 397) has completion paperwork you should keep in good order for immigration purposes; and questions about CPT, OPT, visas, and travel signatures go to **Texas Global / ISSS**, not the program.

## Prospective students

**When should I reach out to faculty?**
Late summer or early fall before the application deadline, once you've read the faculty pages and identified two or three people whose work overlaps yours. Contacting faculty mid-cycle or asking to join a current project rarely helps; funded projects run 12–24 months and are usually fully staffed.

**Is there a part-time or online option?**
No. The PhD is full-time and in residence.

**How is funding decided?**
The program admits only students it can fund for four years, typically through TA and GRA appointments of 20 hours per week. Funding is competitive and cohorts are small (two to five students a year).

**Where do graduates go?**
Mostly to faculty positions, with some in research institutes and public agencies.

**What is the application deadline?**
See the [School of Architecture admissions pages](https://soa.utexas.edu/graduate-admissions/planning-phd) for the current deadline; for fall 2027 admission it is January 3, 2027.
