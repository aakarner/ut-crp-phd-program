## 1. Purpose and Expectations

The dissertation defense is the final milestone for PhD candidates. If successful, it is the last major step required for completing the PhD. The defense is a formal public event during which the PhD candidate presents their dissertation results to their committee, wider faculty, fellow students, and any interested public. Upon completion of the defense, the committee chooses among three outcomes: to evaluate the dissertation as "pass," to a request the candidate "re-defend" the dissertation, or to "fail" the dissertation as "unsatisfactory."

The dissertation defense may be scheduled once the candidate has submitted a full dissertation draft to their committee chair (advisor) and entire committee and once the chair and committee support scheduling the defense as a next step.

The primary purpose of the dissertation defense is to assess whether the student has completed the dissertation research and writing to the committee's satisfaction. Crystal clear communication between the student, their chair, and all committee members is crucial at this juncture. Before scheduling the defense, it is essential for the student and their chair (advisor) to communicate with all committee members and to ensure that all members support scheduling the defense as a next step. Any committee requests for significant additions or revisions to the dissertation research, analysis, or writing should be aired and addressed to the committee's satisfaction before the student schedules the defense. Taking these steps increases the likelihood of a successful defense for the candidate and decreases the likelihood of a committee decision for "redefend" or "fail."

Taking the recommended steps also allows the defense to fulfill a second but equally essential purpose: to allow for constructive, insightful, and forward-looking dialogue about the work among the candidate and full committee, and other faculty and students in attendance. The dissertation is the culmination of years of hard work; the defense ideally allows the PhD candidate to engage thoughtfully with their committee members and wider audience about the work; its execution, potential limitations, and significance; avenues for future research; and any final improvements that may be considered prior to filing the final document.

## 2. Recommended Schedule

Candidates should schedule the defense keeping relevant Graduate School deadlines in mind. It is the candidate's responsibility to inform themselves of relevant Graduate School deadlines for (a) filing the required "Doctoral Graduation Application" during the appropriate period for the semester of graduation; and (b) scheduling the defense before the final cut-off in order for a degree to be granted in a given semester (typically at least two weeks before the last class day of a given semester):

<https://gradschool.utexas.edu/navigating/graduation/deadlines>

When a successful defense is conducted after the Graduate School's cut-off date in a given semester, the degree will not be awarded until the following semester. Defenses may be scheduled in the summer, provided the CRP Program Director, the student's chair (advisor), and the entire committee are available to administer the defense.

## 3. Format of the Dissertation Defense

The student and all members of the student's dissertation committee must be present at the defense. Extenuating circumstances may make virtual attendance important for an individual committee member, and the Graduate School does allows for the dissertation defense to take place virtually. However, the CRP PhD program strongly recommends that the student, dissertation chair (advisor), and committee work to schedule an in-person defense. The in-person defense is more likely to facilitate the constructive, insightful, and forward-looking conversation about the work that the program aims for in its defense proceedings.

Students and their chair should prepare for the defense paperwork in advance, coordinating with the UTSOA Graduate Program Coordinator to ensure that defense forms are ready to go in DocuSign.

The defense typically unfolds as follow: After brief introductions, the candidate gives a presentation of the dissertation results lasting roughly 35 to 40 minutes. Following the presentation, committee members will ask the candidate questions. Once the candidate has answered all of the committee's questions, other faculty in attendance can ask questions of their own, followed by students and others in attendance. Following question-and-answer portion, the public-facing phase of the defense will end, and all non-committee members will be excused so that the committee can conduct its deliberations. Typically, this happens about 1.5 hours into the defense. After its deliberations, the committee communicates the defense result to the student.

## 4. Dissertation Defense Outcomes

There are three possible evaluation decisions for the doctoral defense. All decisions—with the exception of "fail"—must be unanimous.

**i. Pass**

"Pass" requires that both the defense and dissertation are acceptable. In some cases, the committee may require revisions, to be checked by the entire committee or by the supervising professor only, following the options below and as agreed upon at the time of the defense and communicated with the student. The options are:

a. Pass with no revisions.  
b. Pass with minor revisions. (The chair provides the student a written account of the requested revisions. The student makes the revisions requested, and **they are approved by the chair. Other committee members are involved only if the chair seeks their input** as to whether the revisions are adequate.)  
c. Pass with major revisions. (The chair provides the student a written account of the requested revisions. The student makes the revisions requested, and **they must be separately reviewed and approved by the chair and one or more other committee members, up to and including the full committee, at the discretion of the committee.**)

**ii. "Re-defend"**

If the dissertation and/or defense are not acceptable to all members of the committee, the decision will change to either "re-defend" or "fail" as discussed below. "Re-defend" indicates that the committee is not satisfied with the dissertation or with the oral examination, but believes that rewriting may make it acceptable.

**iii. Fail**

"Fail" indicates that at least one member of the committee has decided that the dissertation is unsatisfactory and may not be rewritten. This decision normally results in the termination of a doctoral student's program.

## 5. Dissertation Defense Paperwork

Upon completion of the defense, the student and their chair (advisor) are responsible for ensuring that the committee's official recommendation is communicated to the Graduate School on the Report of Dissertation Committee. It is essential to complete this form and return it in a timely fashion.

## 6. Important Reminders for Students and Chairs (Advisors)

i. Students planning to defend their dissertation must be registered for any CRP \*99W class during that final semester.

ii. Upon confirming a dissertation defense date and time when the chair (advisor), committee, and PhD Program Director can be physically present, the student should send a formal calendar invite to all.

iii. Students must book a room for their defense with the UTSOA front desk. Tel. 512-471-1922. If it is available, the Dean's Conference Room ("DCR") is a favored venue for the defense. Once you have confirmed a room, update the calendar invite with location for all. Students are encouraged to invite friends and colleagues to the defense as well.

iv. Students must submit their complete dissertation, including abstract, to the entire Dissertation Committee and the CRP PhD Program Director at least four weeks before the defense date.

v. At this time, four weeks before defense, the PhD Program Director will announce the upcoming defense to CRP faculty and students and to the wider CRP community.

vi. Once you have submitted your dissertation to the full Committee, your chair **must** send a follow-up communication to the committee members, as suggested below. This kind of message is required because it can help to alert the chair before the defense to any serious reservations from committee members about moving forward. The student is strongly encouraged to communicate with the Chair to ensure that the Chair sends this communication.

> *Dear [committee member names],*
>
> *I am happy to share [STUDENT]'s dissertation for your review prior to the defense scheduled on [DATE]. [STUDENT] will be following up with details about the physical location soon. I've also cc'd [Dr. XXXXX] here, who is PhD Director for our program.*
>
> *I will follow up the week before [STUDENT]'s defense date to see if any of you have major concerns that might prevent the defense from going forward as planned. Minor corrections and changes are commonplace, but given the time and effort of a doctoral course of study I feel the defense should not go forward with someone having major concerns (e.g. requiring the student to re-do portions of the analysis or to add significant, new analyses or elements). My preference is to delay the defense and have the candidate address such problems before a defense goes forward.*
>
> *I think [STUDENT's] dissertation has [insert chair's assessment of dissertation findings], and I look forward to discussing them with you at the defense. Please reach out if you have any questions or concerns before then.*
>
> *Thank you all again for serving on [STUDENT]'s committee and for the guidance and input that you have shared with [STUDENT] throughout their doctoral studies.*
>
> *Best,*  
> *[DISSERTATION CHAIR]*

vii. Immediately after submitting you dissertation to the committee -- and **at least two weeks before your scheduled defense** – submit the "Request for Final Oral Examination" form to the Graduate School, signed by all of your committee members. **The signature requirements for this form mean you cannot leave it until the last minute. It will take a while to get everyone to sign, so it's recommended to start immediately after submitting your dissertation to the committee.**

viii. One week before the defense date, your chair **must** send a follow up message to the entire **committee**. **Again, such a message is required because it may help to alert the chair before the defense to any serious reservations from committee members about moving forward.** The student is strongly encouraged to communicate with the Chair to ensure that the Chair sends this communication.

> *Dear [committee member names],*
>
> *I am emailing to checking in on [STUDENT]'s dissertation reviews.*
>
> *Please email me if you have any significant concerns that would lead me to possibly rescheduling the defense because of the time need to address such concerns.*
>
> *If you could reply in the next few days with your assessment that all is in order, from your perspective, to go forward, I would greatly appreciate it.*
>
> *Kind regards,*  
> *[DISSERTATION CHAIR]*
