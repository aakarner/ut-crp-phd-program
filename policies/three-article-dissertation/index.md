## 1. Introduction

PhD students in Community and Regional Planning may choose to write a traditional monograph style dissertation or a dissertation consisting of three articles. A traditional monograph approach typically results in a book-length manuscript. A three-article approach yields three scholarly papers developed around a common research problem and integrated thematically. As this approach is newer in the planning field, this guidance is intended to establish clear expectations for three-article dissertation. Along with the three articles themselves, the three-article dissertation would include an introduction and a conclusion chapter that synthesize the overarching research problem and articulate the overarching argument and contributions of the three articles.

A three-article dissertation approach may help in some cases to position students to publish their research soon following graduation or prior to graduation. Such an approach may also help students develop a successful publishing strategy early on, which is especially important given the pressure to publish in peer-reviewed journals during the PhD program.

A three-article dissertation is not a shortcut to a doctoral degree. It is an option that presents its own challenges and opportunities. The need for three articles should be clear and approved by the dissertation committee, and the three articles should not merely represent minor tweaks of a work that would be more appropriately reported in just one or two articles. Students must carefully consider the advantages and disadvantages of the two approaches in consultation with their academic advisor and mentors. The purpose of this policy is to clarify the process to follow for three-article dissertations.

## 2. Summary Schedule

**Prior to the proposal defense:** Choose either the traditional monograph or the three-article dissertation approach in consultation with your committee.

**During the proposal defense:** In addition to the typical proposal content, you must provide specific information about your proposed articles. See Section 3, below.

**During the research and writing phase:** If you wish to submit one or more of your articles for publication prior to graduation, follow the guidelines below regarding copyright, authorship, and journal selection and review.

**Prior to the dissertation defense:** In addition to developing the three articles, write a comprehensive introduction chapter and a comprehensive conclusion chapter that clearly articulate the overarching research problem, the overarching argument, and the collective contributions of the three articles. See Section 8 below for more detailed requirements.

## 3. Dissertation Proposal Requirements

Students should decide as early as possible, in concert with their dissertation chair, whether to pursue the three-article dissertation format. However, they may switch from one format to the other prior to scheduling the proposal defense or following the proposal defense, provided their dissertation committee approves the change. Students who defended a traditional proposal and wish, post-defense, to switch to the three-article format must revise their proposal following the guidelines in this document; submit the revised proposal to the committee for approval; and inform the PhD program director of the change in writing, including a copy of the revised proposal.

The three-article dissertation alters the format, but not the content, expected of PhD level research. Students are still solely responsible for articulating an overarching research problem, developing an overarching research design, conducting all the research and analysis, and writing the analysis of results and conclusions. The three-article dissertation proposal must therefore include the typical content required for a traditional dissertation, including a problem statement; review of the relevant topical literature; review of the relevant theoretical literature; a justification for the chosen research design; a description and justification of research design and methods; a statement of the significance of the proposed research for theory and practice; and a timeline for completion of the dissertation research.

In addition, in a three-article dissertation proposal, you must also include for deliberation and approval by your dissertation committee a concise summary (no more than 2-pages) for \*each\* of the three articles. Each 2-page article summary will include, in brief summary form:

a. Tentative title of the article.  
b. Author and, if applicable, proposed co-authors. For any co-author, the student must explain their role in the research and contribution to the article.  
c. An outline of the article.  
d. A summary of the empirical content.  
e. A summary of your argument and/or hypothesis.  
f. Explanation of how the article will contribute to the overarching literature, research problem, and theoretical discussion presented in the dissertation proposal.  
g. Justification of why the material to be included merits its own stand-alone publication.  
h. Discussion and justification of any overlap with the other proposed articles.  
i. Selected journal and justification for the selection.  
j. In case of proposed co-authors, a justification and explanation of their contributions, including the likely percentage effort they will contribute.  
k. Timeline for the development of the article.

After the proposal defense, as the dissertation research progresses, students may need to make changes to a proposed article. Changes can be made to any of the three manuscripts before the dissertation defense, provided the student makes the dissertation committee members aware of the proposed changes and their rationale, and provided the committee approves the changes.

## 4. Cases of Articles Published Before the Dissertation Proposal

a. Subject to approval of the dissertation committee, the students may include in their dissertation \*one\* article published prior to the proposal defense. In such cases, students must address in the proposal and proposal defense the potential inclusion of this article, and the committee must approve its inclusion as one of the three required dissertation articles. The article must be based upon research completed while the student is enrolled in the CRP PhD program.  
b. The published article must satisfy the requirements for authorship listed below in Section 6 and meet committees standards for journal rigor discussed below in Section 7.  
c. In the case of a previously published article, the student must also address points in Section 3 above to the satisfaction of the committee. The discussion of the points in Section 3 will reflect the fact that the article has been published and is not "tentative."  
d. If the committee wants revisions to a paper that is already published, such as additional data, further data analysis, a longer literature review, or further theory development, the candidate must complete these revisions to satisfy the committee prior to submitting the dissertation.  
e. In cases where previously published articles have been co-authored and they comply with the requirements listed elsewhere in this policy, it is the student's responsibility to ensure the co-authors are willing and able to sign the "Co-Author Permission Form" required by the Graduate School.  
f. Students must consult with their academic advisor and mentors to ensure publications produced prior to the proposal defense meet these criteria.

## 5. Copyright

a. The copyright to articles generally belongs to the journal. The Graduate School of the University of Texas requires that "When papers or articles that have been previously published are included, the dissertation must include permission(s) of the copyright holder(s) for reproduction in the dissertation" (<https://catalog.utexas.edu/graduate/degree-requirements/doctor-of-philosophy/>). Students must communicate directly with the Graduate School to ensure that copyright restrictions are satisfied and demonstrate to the committee's satisfaction that the article can be included in the dissertation without violating the journal's or UT's copyright restrictions.

b. When asking the journal for permission to include the article in the dissertation, students should notify the journal editor that the dissertation will be made available on-line because uploading the dissertation to the Texas Digital Libraries at UT is a requirement of graduation (<https://gradschool.utexas.edu/academics/theses-and-dissertations/digital-submission-requirement>). Students may include this language from the UT Graduate School in their communication with the journal:

> "Graduating students are required to publish their thesis, report, dissertation or treatise digitally by uploading a single PDF to the Texas Digital Library (TDL). Because electronic theses and dissertations (ETDs) must be published, if any material contained in your document might require permission from another party for publication, all such permissions must be obtained prior to the submission of the ETD."

## 6. Authorship

a. The PhD candidate must be first and corresponding author on all articles included in the dissertation.  
b. Only one article to be included in the dissertation may include co-authors.  
c. Any co-authors must be identified in the student's dissertation proposal and their inclusion and contribution must be explained and justified during the proposal defense. The article and the role of the co-authors must be approved by all members of the dissertation committee. The student must clarify the percentage of effort and description of the role played by each author in the introductory chapter to the dissertation. The student's PhD committee must approve any changes made to co-authorship and/or the order of names and/or degree of contribution of co-authors prior to graduation.  
d. Co-authorship with other students in the UT Community and Regional Planning Program is not permitted on articles included in a dissertation. Co-authorship with other students outside the UT Community and Regional Planning Program may be permitted by the PhD committee, but only with signed permission of the other student and acknowledgment of the other student regarding their degree of contribution.  
e. In cases where articles have been co-authored and they comply with the requirements listed elsewhere in this policy, it is the student's responsibility to ensure the co-authors are willing and able to sign the "Co-Author Permission Form" required by the Graduate School.

## 7. Journal Selection & Review

a. Journals vary in their foci but also in the rigor of their peer review, their reputation, and their reach and audience. The PhD committee will serve as an "editorial board" for the student and help select reputable journals that will challenge the student and offer a reasonable chance of publication success.  
b. For previously published articles that a student requests to include in the dissertation at the dissertation proposal stage, the committee may decline to approve the inclusion of an article or book chapter if it does not meet the committee's standards.  
c. For articles that are proposed in the dissertation proposal, the dissertation proposal must clearly indicate what journals have been chosen for manuscript submission and justify their selection.  
d. It is not necessary for a manuscript to be accepted by the journal to be included in the dissertation or for the dissertation to be successfully defended.  
e. If journal reviewers suggest revisions to any of the three submitted manuscripts prior to the dissertation defense, reviewer comments and the student's plan for addressing those suggestions must be shared with dissertation committee members before completing revisions. This allows the dissertation committee to ensure that any review and revision process is fruitful and meets overarching goals of the dissertation research. Students may choose to defer completing revisions until their dissertation has been successfully defended.  
f. The journal peer-review process does not substitute for the proposal approval and defense process. An article accepted or published prior to the dissertation defense will be subject to the same level of scrutiny by the student's dissertation committee as the other articles or a traditional monograph dissertation. The committee may request revisions to a published article or reject a published article. The student is therefore expected to communicate clearly with the committee during the doctoral program to ensure that the work produced meets the standards of the dissertation committee.

## 8. Dissertation Content

a. The dissertation must include a minimum of three articles. These articles should form a cohesive body of work supporting a theme or themes that are expressed clearly in the introduction (Chapter 1).  
b. The dissertation must include an abstract that synthesizes the articles, as well as an introduction (Chapter 1) and a Conclusion (the chapter following the third article) that synthesizes and discusses what was found and argued in the three articles.  
c. The introduction (Chapter 1) should function to tie the various manuscripts together and describe their collective meaning and combined contribution to the field. It should include: a) a definition or statement of the research question(s); b) the importance, i.e., why the question(s) need to be researched; c) the theoretical foundation(s) of this body of research; d) an overview of the important literature (a very general overview because each article will have its own unique literature review); e) the research question(s); and f) the methodology or methodologies.  
d. The conclusion chapter should tie the three chapters together and demonstrate how the various manuscripts together contribute to a single body of knowledge. The conclusion should briefly summarize the major findings and limitations of the work as a whole before proceeding to a discussion of the overarching findings and recommendations for future work, planning practice or policy, and/or planning-related action more broadly. You must present and discuss linkages (i.e., similarities and differences) between the separate manuscripts in the dissertation in order to demonstrate that the articles present different dimensions of a coherent body of work. If any chapters are published, you should discuss any indications of response to the work, including referencing or response from other scholars or planners. Also discuss how the three articles, taken together, reveal lingering knowledge gaps and necessities for future research.  
e. Students are responsible for informing themselves about and complying with UT dissertation requirements discussed above and format guidelines, as specified by The Graduate School in its Format Guidelines for Dissertations, Treatises, Theses, and Reports: <https://gradschool.utexas.edu/academics/theses-and-dissertations/digital-submission-requirement> (see Formatting > Dissertation Templates). Also follow the specific instructions for a 3-article dissertation, available at <https://utexas.box.com/shared/static/ffag6al7b0eaaqcheml6dcot9u3z6zit.pdf>. See in particular Section VI.e, as cited below:

> **FORMAT GUIDELINES FOR DISSERTATIONS, TREATISES, THESES AND REPORTS, Section VI.e**
>
> "Dissertations containing one or more papers or articles must include brief introductions and conclusions that put the work in context and an acknowledgement of any previous publication of each paper in another dissertation or other venue. In the case of multi-authored papers, students are allowed to include any portion of multi-authored works in their ETD only after receiving the approval of all other co-authors by obtaining their signatures on the supplemental page to either the Master's Committee Approval form or the Report of Dissertation Committee. Additionally, a statement must be included explaining the contribution of the dissertator to each paper. The contribution statement might include, for example, information about the dissertator's contribution to designing research, performing research, contributing new reagents or analytic tools, analyzing data, writing the dissertation or other area-specific classification of research activities. When papers or articles that have been previously published are included, the dissertation must include permission(s) of the copyright holder(s) for reproduction in the dissertation. The supervisor and dissertation committee will review the stated contributions and must be satisfied that the dissertator's collective contribution to the multiple-authored papers or articles is sufficient to represent a dissertation."
