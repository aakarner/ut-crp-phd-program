# What Changed: Comprehensive Exam Policy Revision (Draft, September 2026)

This page summarizes how the [revised draft](index.md) differs from the [policy adopted in July 2025](2025-07-adopted.md). A line-by-line comparison is available as a [side-by-side diff](diff.html), but because the revision reorganizes the document, that view overstates the change. The substance is below.

## Changes in substance (need GSC discussion)

**1. A "Who Does What" section (new Section 2).** The adopted policy scatters responsibilities across nine sections. The revision collects them in one place, by role: student, advisor (exam committee chair), committee members, core-course faculty, and PhD Program Coordinator. The organizing principle is that the advisor and committee *prepare and grade* the exam and the PhD Program Coordinator *administers* it. This reflects the September 2026 discussion among current and past coordinators (Sletto, Wegmann, Sciara, Zhang, Karner).

**2. Essays 1 and 2 are written and graded by the core-course faculty for every student.** The adopted policy says the committee chair develops questions for all four essays and that core-course faculty are "expected to develop questions relevant to the courses taught" and must also grade Essays 1 and 2. The revision states current practice explicitly: the faculty teaching Planning Theory and Research Design write and grade Essays 1 and 2 for every student, whether or not they sit on the committee; questions are newly written for each student rather than recycled; and the advisor leads on Essays 3 and 4.

**3. Essays 1 and 2 should connect to the student's area.** New language in Section 1 and Section 7 says theory and research design questions should engage the field through the student's specialization and dissertation interests, and explains why (usable writing for the dissertation; students think about how the field applies to their work). The adopted policy is silent on this.

**4. Advising norm.** Section 2 adds one sentence: taking on a doctoral advisee means committing to lead that student's comprehensive exam process. This is a statement of expectation rather than a procedural rule.

**5. Special Field approval.** The adopted policy says a student-designed specialization "must be approved by the CRP PhD Committee," a body that does not exist by that name. The revision says CRP-GSC. If a smaller body (e.g., the PhD Program Coordinator and advisor) is intended, that should be stated.

**6. Section 11 (unsatisfactory outcomes) consolidated.** The adopted Section 9 contains two near-duplicate paragraphs covering the paths to a full CRP-GSC deliberation (consensus reached that the student failed; consensus not reached). The revision merges them into one paragraph with the same outcomes and the same majority-vote requirement for termination. Worth a careful read to confirm nothing was lost.

**7. Results-letter timing made explicit.** The adopted policy implies (via the checklist) that students hear results within five weeks of submitting; the revision states it in the policy schedule.

## Changes in form (editorial)

- **Title standardized to "PhD Program Coordinator."** The adopted text uses this in most places but also "CRP Program Coordinator" (Section 9). All references now use one title. "CRP Program Director" (the head of the CRP program) is unchanged, as it is a different role.
- **Schedule presented as one table** with the three exam periods side by side, rather than three repeated lists. Dates are unchanged.
- **Grading responsibilities presented as a table** (who grades which essay). Content unchanged from the adopted bullets.
- **Sections reordered** so the document follows the exam chronologically: purpose, roles, schedule, committee, specialization, reading lists, questions, exam period, grading, revisions, unsatisfactory outcomes.
- **Typos corrected:** "retake the exam the exam sequence" (twice), "chair comprehensive exam committee chair," "Community and regional Planning," "CH√CKLIST."
- **Checklist updated** to include the winter exam period (the adopted checklist offers only fall and spring), to list the committee-roster and abstract steps, and to match the revised numbering.
- **Status header added** at the top of the document (status, what it supersedes, who prepared it) so readers can tell an adopted policy from a draft.

## Unchanged

The PhD Program Coordinator continues to receive all grades and comments, synthesize them into the results letter, and communicate results to the student. Also unchanged: committee composition (four members, chair from CRP-GSC, at least one external); the four essay areas; the list of specializations; all deadlines and the 10-day exam window; the grade scale; the one-round-of-revisions rule; the two-week silent-consent rule for revised essays; external members' grades being advisory; and the majority vote of the CRP-GSC required to terminate a student's program.
