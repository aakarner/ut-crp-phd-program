# What Changed: Comprehensive Exam Policy Revision (Draft, September 2026)

This page summarizes how the [revised draft](index.md) differs from the [policy adopted in July 2025](2025-07-adopted.md). A line-by-line comparison is available as a [side-by-side diff](diff.html), but because the revision reorganizes the document, that view overstates the change. The substance is below.

## Changes in substance (need GSC discussion)

**1. A "Who Does What" section (new Section 2).** The adopted policy scatters responsibilities across nine sections. The revision collects them in one place, by role: student, advisor (exam committee chair), committee members, core-course faculty, and PhD Program Coordinator. The organizing principle is that the advisor and committee *prepare* the exam and *grade individual essays*, and the PhD Program Coordinator *administers* the exam, *synthesizes* the grades, and resolves inconsistencies. This reflects the September 2026 discussion among current and past coordinators (Sletto, Wegmann, Sciara, Zhang, Karner).

**2. Fixed exam periods replaced by a flexible timeline.** The adopted policy defines three exam periods (spring/early summer, late summer/fall, winter) with calendar deadlines for reading lists (e.g., January 15 / February 15) and questions (April 15, July 30, December 1). In practice students schedule the exam when it works for them and their committee, usually near the start or end of a semester, and the calendar deadlines have not been enforced. The revision drops the fixed periods and states the lead times instead: draft reading lists about three months before the exam, final lists about two months before, questions at least two weeks before, with the start date set by the student, advisor, and PhD Program Coordinator. The grading, results, and revision windows after the exam (4 weeks / 5 weeks / 4 weeks / 2 weeks) are unchanged.

**3. Question structure and sourcing written down.** The adopted policy never says how many questions the exam contains; it reads as if there are exactly four. The revision states current practice: each of the four areas carries two or three questions, the student answers one per area, and questions must draw on the student's final reading list. On sourcing, the adopted policy says the chair develops questions for all four essays, core-course faculty are "expected to develop questions relevant to the courses taught," and all CRP-GSC members are "invited and encouraged" to propose questions. The revision keeps all of that and makes it concrete: the Planning Theory and Research Design instructors always contribute questions for Areas 1 and 2 (and grade those essays) for every student, whether or not they sit on the committee; the advisor drafts questions for all four areas, with particular responsibility for Areas 3 and 4; CRP-GSC members of the committee are expected to propose questions in all four areas, matching the fact that they already grade all four (the adopted policy has the chair "solicit questions from other committee members" without saying which areas); external members propose in Areas 3 and/or 4; the PhD Program Coordinator circulates the final reading list to the full CRP-GSC with an invitation to propose questions in any area; and questions are newly written for each student rather than recycled.

**4. Essays 1 and 2 should connect to the student's area.** New language in Section 1 and Section 7 says theory and research design questions should engage the field through the student's specialization and dissertation interests, and explains why (usable writing for the dissertation; students think about how the field applies to their work). The adopted policy is silent on this.

**5. Advising norm.** Section 2 adds one sentence: taking on a doctoral advisee means committing to lead that student's comprehensive exam process. This is a statement of expectation rather than a procedural rule.

**6. Special Field approval.** The adopted policy says a student-designed specialization "must be approved by the CRP PhD Committee," a body that does not exist by that name. The revision says CRP-GSC. If a smaller body (e.g., the PhD Program Coordinator and advisor) is intended, that should be stated.

**7. Section 11 (unsatisfactory outcomes) consolidated.** The adopted Section 9 contains two near-duplicate paragraphs covering the paths to a full CRP-GSC deliberation (consensus reached that the student failed; consensus not reached). The revision merges them into one paragraph with the same outcomes and the same majority-vote requirement for termination. Worth a careful read to confirm nothing was lost.

**8. Results-letter timing made explicit.** The adopted policy implies (via the checklist) that students hear results within five weeks of submitting; the revision states it in the policy schedule.

## Changes in form (editorial)

- **Title standardized to "PhD Program Coordinator."** The adopted text uses this in most places but also "CRP Program Coordinator" (Section 9). All references now use one title. "CRP Program Director" (the head of the CRP program) is unchanged, as it is a different role.
- **Timeline presented as one table** of lead times relative to the exam date (see substantive change 2), rather than three lists of calendar dates.
- **Grading responsibilities presented as a table** (who grades which essay). Content unchanged from the adopted bullets.
- **Sections reordered** so the document follows the exam chronologically: purpose, roles, schedule, committee, specialization, reading lists, questions, exam period, grading, revisions, unsatisfactory outcomes.
- **Typos corrected:** "retake the exam the exam sequence" (twice), "chair comprehensive exam committee chair," "Community and regional Planning," "CH√CKLIST."
- **Checklist updated** to use lead times instead of calendar deadlines, to record the intended exam date, and to list the committee-roster and abstract steps.
- **Status header added** at the top of the document (status, what it supersedes, who prepared it) so readers can tell an adopted policy from a draft.

## Unchanged

The PhD Program Coordinator continues to receive all grades and comments, synthesize them into the results letter, and communicate results to the student. Also unchanged: committee composition (four members, chair from CRP-GSC, at least one external); the four essay areas; the list of specializations; the 10-day exam window and all post-exam windows (grading, results, revisions); the grade scale; the one-round-of-revisions rule; the two-week silent-consent rule for revised essays; external members' grades being advisory; and the majority vote of the CRP-GSC required to terminate a student's program.
